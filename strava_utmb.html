Training data analysis to complete the Ultra-Trail du Mont-Blanc

Project Overview

The UTMB (Ultra-Trail du Mont-Blanc) is the most prestigious trail race in the world. To finish it, athletes must cover 171 kilometers with over 10,000 meters of elevation gain around Mont Blanc, crossing Italy, Switzerland, and France within 46.5 hours.

I started trail running in 2016, though I have been logging all my training data on Strava since 2013. Completing my first 100 km race sparked the ambition to train for the UTMB. In 2022, I crossed the finish line in 40 hours and 43 minutes. Having achieved my dream race, I decided to analyze my training data to see what it took to get there.

I exported all my training data from Strava. I then cleaned and prepared the dataset in Microsoft Power BI, limiting the data up to the end of 2022, before conducting the analysis.

Cleaned Strava activities dataset in Microsoft Power BI.

Training Volume & Progression

Training data was analyzed, considering only running sessions, from May 2013 (the start of running workouts) until the end of December 2022. In total, 1355 running training workouts were recorded.

17276 km total distance
439.6 km total elevation gain
1981 h total elapsed time

In the graph below showing annual training volume, it is evident that during the early years both distance and elevation gain were very low compared to the final years of the analysis. This is mainly explained by two factors: on one hand, in the early years of running, the body was still gradually adapting to longer distances, as anything over 15 km used to result in joint discomfort; on the other hand, at that point I had no idea I would one day attempt the UTMB, so building up training volume was never part of the plan.

Progression was consistent in both distance and elevation gain. However, from 2018 to 2022, there was a significantly sharper increase in elevation gain—up by 67% (53,867 → 90,069 m)—compared to distance, which increased by 48% (1,878 → 2,773 km). This shifted the average elevation gain from 28.7 m per kilometer to 32.5 m per kilometer.

This shift is explained by my difficulty on longer climbs, as my usual training grounds are not in high-mountain areas. As I took on longer races, I realized I needed to increase my overall elevation gain to better adapt to those conditions.

COVID-19 Period Analysis

Although progression was steady, it is worth noting that there was a lockdown period associated with the COVID-19 pandemic. While mobility restrictions were strictly enforced for only a few months, the period from March 2020 to October 2021 was considered a major constraint, particularly on participation in trail races—during this timeframe, I took part in only 5 races adapted to pandemic guidelines. Despite these limitations, the table below highlights that during the pandemic period I was able to dedicate more time to training, logging greater distance and elevation gain than in the equivalent preceding period. While there was more available time, it required significantly greater self-discipline, as motivation was tested without group workouts or scheduled races on the calendar.

The most significant difference was in the number of workouts exceeding 42 km. This was primarily due to the sharp drop in race participation, as training sessions beyond marathon distance presented considerably greater logistical challenges.

As shown in the chart below, in 2020 there was a drastic reduction in longer workouts, with progression steadily resuming in the post-pandemic period.

Final Preparation for the UTMB (2022)

In the final year of analysis, 2022—when the UTMB took place—the highest number of races over 100 km was recorded. This served as the final preparation phase to test and refine critical race-day factors, such as digestion and nutrient absorption, sleep management and exhaustion, nighttime gear, and the prevention of blisters and chafing. Fortunately, it was a successful season with no DNFs, which significantly bolstered confidence given the demanding nature of each race. The events were evenly spaced at 8-week intervals, which proved ideal for recovery and build-up in my case. A clear tapering period is evident in the two weeks preceding each race.

Highs, Lows & Lessons Learned

This entire training journey had its highs and lows. A key milestone was completing my first ultra-trail (distances above 42 km), which only happened in 2018. In 2019 came the first true test of resilience: after a DNF in an 80 km race, I managed to finish my first 100 km race the very next month—an unforgettable milestone. It is a continuous learning process of understanding your body and identifying where mistakes happen in order to push beyond your limits.

With my focus set on the UTMB, I attempted a 100-mile race in the Pyrenees, but the outcome fell short of expectations. Following that setback, the dream of one day completing the UTMB took a heavy blow, and I almost threw in the towel. Fortunately, 2022 proved to be a resounding success, and I was able to turn that dream into reality.
